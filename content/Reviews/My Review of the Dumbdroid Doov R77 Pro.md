## Backstory
I've missed my old Nokia 2330 Classic ever since I had to part with it during high school, since the battery gave up. My parents forced a smartphone into my hand back then and I was SO AGAINST IT!
I never liked the size, heft and fragility of the things.
So, when my Oneplus 6Ts replaced screen was on its last legs, I found the Dumbdroid project on Reddit and decided to give it a try. And that's a decision that I don't regret.
Since I was part of the release day buyer (or rather a few days after release) it took some time to get mine, but I have no problem with waiting. 

## Review
The setup was easy and I'll admit, I was skeptical if this would be a good phone for me. Since most of us are so used to smart phones, getting used to a physical keyboard can be a relearning curve. I got into it quite quickly, surprised how much my fingers remembered my old Nokia keyboards.
The phone sits well in my hand and is very lightweight. 

Many of my friends "ooh" when I show them the phone, some even wants to buy one for themselves! And the price is great, 1200kr (including shipping!) is *nothing* for a phone nowadays.

It's easy to use and supports Play Store and F droid so you can install mostly anything you want. 
Though, as designed, I wouldn't recommend installing anything more social media-eque than Whatsapp. Speaking of apps.

Most apps work well enough on its small screen. I've found a few that work less well and a one that crashes if you use the physical keyboard.
- Postnord does NOT work well when showing the QR code. It renders super small, since they seem to have hard-coded the QR code screen to render that way.
- BankID works but MUST use it's own on-screen keyboard to type your PIN. If you type it using the TT9 keyboard, the app crashes and restarts. That's not the fault of the phone though, BankID uses it's own proprietary keyboard.
 
I do have one nitpick when typing a phone number to call. The "End Call" button works to end a call, but the "Start / Answer Call" button does NOT work to start the call when I've typed out the number. It's only a small thing but if a future update to either the phone app or TT9 could fix it, that would be AWESOME!

All in all, it's a great small phone that is what the Nokia of yesteryear would be today. I love it and strongly recommend it to anyone who is disappointed with the massive, heavy tablets people call phones today. Its small size, light weight and ease of use just makes it amazing. And you get Bluetooth as well! Though I would NOT throw it around as my schoolmates did with their old dumb phones that were as tough as nails. 

### Pros
1. **SUPERB** form factor! It sits so well in my small hands! And I can operate it with only one hand! How many modern phones can you say that about, if you don't have giant bear hands with long fingers! 
2. A lovely keyboard! Feels great to type on, very customizable using the TT9 app. (One note on the app though, mine fails to update using Play Store so I have to sideload every update.)
3. Great battery life. Since I don't scroll or play games on this thing (didn't do on my old phone either....) the battery easily goes two or three days on a single charge. 
4.  Dual SIM support (one is dual purpose and can be used as a SD card slot as well)
5. Lightweight, it weighs nearly nothing!
6. The Dumbdroid quick settings panel ain't sexy but it's utilitarian and I like it! Easy access to volumes and sound profiles.
7. THE PRICE! With phones costing as much as a good used car, this phone is a steal! 1200kr (about 110-120€) it's almost robbery. You get a lovely phone for the money!
8. You get a protective case with the phone!
9. Symfonium works superbly on it!
### Cons
Some of these might be considered nitpicks, others are probably actual bugs
1. When making a call I think the volume of the in-ear speaker is to high. I've tried to turn it down but even at it's lowest, it's way to high for my liking. So I end up holding the phone a good 70-80mm from my ear. 
2. Audio over the USB C port ain't supported. But for me personally, when I got this phone I also picked up a Sony Walkman so it's not to bad.
3. This might be more of a general Android thing than this phone especially but I don't like that the screen (kind of randomly) turns on when I lift the phone. I've dug through the settings but there doesn't seem to be a way to turn this behavior off. 
4. Speaking of the screen, if I turn the brightness down any lower then around 50%, the backlight goes crazy and starts to flash like a maniac. I'm not versed in screen tech enough to speculate on what's causing it, but I note that enabling the option "Extra Dim" when on around 50% brightness allows it to dim down quite a great amount *without* having to go further down the brightness slider. 
5. No physical volume buttons
